# LinkedIn & Cold Outreach Tells (Codex v4.2, Part 6.2; triad rule updated in v4.3)

Companion to `blacklist.md`. Apply when writing or editing LinkedIn posts. The cold-email section is included because it's the same source section and the tells overlap — skip it if the piece isn't outreach.

## LinkedIn post tells

- **The "Rocket Launcher" format** — one emoji per bullet point (🚀 for growth, 💡 for innovation, 🔒 for security, etc.). Delete the emoji-as-bullet-decorator pattern entirely. If a list is genuinely needed, use plain dashes or fold it into a sentence — see the general Bold-Colon/labeled-listicle ban in `blacklist.md`, which applies here too.
- **The engagement-bait close** — "Agree? ♻️ Repost if this resonated with you," or any variant asking for reactions/reposts/comments as a formula. Cut it. If the post is good, it doesn't need to ask.
- **"I." in isolation on its own line** — a lone "I." as a stylistic hook-opener, common in ghostwritten LinkedIn content. Not banned because it's inherently bad, but it's become a template move — use it only if the post genuinely opens with a first-person confession or scene, per the general "say the unpredictable thing" rule, not as a reflexive formatting tic.
- **Three takeaways, uniform part of speech** – the reflexive triad (P08) applied to closing-summary bullets. The tell is the template: three takeaways all starting with the same grammatical form ("Building trust. Driving growth. Scaling fast."). Use as many points as the post actually makes, and vary their form.
- General checks still apply in full: same-start runs, banned words/phrases, no meta-label conclusion ("Key takeaways," "The bottom line"), no summary restatement of the post's own thesis.

## Cold email tells (for reference — only relevant if the piece is outreach, not a LinkedIn post)

- **Em-dash slop** — zero tolerance in emails specifically (stricter than the general 1/500-word rule). Any em dash in a cold email is a hard fail.
- **Over-polished 3-context-1-pain-1-CTA structure** — introduce deliberate imperfection. A short sentence where a long one is expected reads as more human than a perfectly balanced paragraph.
- **Fake personalization** — referencing something stale ("your impressive work at [Company]" pointing at a two-year-old post). Fix: reference something from the current week, or don't personalize at all.
- **Opening with the sender's company name** ("At [OurCo], we believe...") — start with the recipient's problem, not the pitch.
- **"I hope this email finds you well"** — already a hard-fail phrase in `blacklist.md`. Delete; start with the actual reason for writing.
- **Buzzword value prop** ("revolutionize your workflow") — state the specific outcome for a specific customer instead. A number beats an adjective.
